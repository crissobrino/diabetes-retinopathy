CHRONOLOGICAL EVOLUTION OF FINE-TUNING APPROACHES
===================================================

1. BASELINE: first_attempt.ipynb
   Backbone:    EfficientNet-B0 (ImageNet pretrained)
                Unfrozen blocks: features[6,7,8] + classifier
                Head: Dropout(0.3) → Linear(1280, 1)
   Training:    SGD (lr=1e-3, momentum=0.9) + StepLR(step_size=7, gamma=0.1)
                Loss: BCEWithLogitsLoss(pos_weight ≈ 2.7); 20 epochs, patience=5
   Result:      0.698 pub / 0.708 priv
   Notes:       Establishes the floor. AlexNet from the lab template was replaced with
                EfficientNet-B0 (69.8% → 77.1% ImageNet top-1) as the starting backbone.
                Single-stage training. No TTA, no ensemble. SGD with StepLR is suboptimal
                for transfer learning on small datasets: sharp LR drops misalign with the
                cosine decay profile preferred by adaptive optimisers.

--

2. attempt_with_b.ipynb  (Series B)
   Changes:     FT model unchanged from baseline (SGD, StepLR, 20 epochs, patience=5).
                CustomNet optimizer changed to Adam + CosineAnnealingLR (custom-only edit).
   Result:      0.691 pub / 0.693 priv
   Notes:       Deliberate control. FT held constant to isolate the custom optimizer change.
                Slight regression vs baseline is within run-to-run variance.

--

3. attempt_with_a_b.ipynb  (Series A+B)
   Changes:     CLAHETransform() added as first step in both train and val/test pipelines
                (LAB colour space, L-channel only; clipLimit=2.0, tileGridSize=8×8).
                FT architecture and optimizer otherwise unchanged.
   Result:      0.711 pub / 0.691 priv
   Notes:       CLAHE improves the public score (+0.020 vs Series B) but private drops.
                Root cause: CLAHE alters the global intensity distribution. CropByEye uses
                a fixed threshold (0.10) to detect the eye boundary from raw pixel intensity;
                after CLAHE, background pixels can exceed the threshold, corrupting the crop.
                CLAHE is permanently banned from the pipeline after this experiment.

--

4. attempt_with_c.ipynb  (Series C)
   Changes:     EfficientNet optimizer: SGD → AdamW(lr=3e-4, weight_decay=1e-2)
                Scheduler: StepLR → CosineAnnealingLR(T_max=30)
                Epochs: 20 → 30, patience=7
                CLAHE removed.
   Result:      0.751 pub / 0.734 priv  (+0.053 pub from baseline)
   Notes:       Single largest improvement up to this point. AdamW with decoupled weight decay
                avoids the L2/Adam interference that SGD sidesteps only by ignoring second-order
                moments entirely. Cosine annealing provides a smooth, gradual LR reduction
                rather than sudden step drops. AdamW + CosineAnnealingLR is adopted for all
                subsequent fine-tuning experiments.

--

5. second_attempt.ipynb  (Series D–F)
   Changes:     Two-stage progressive unfreezing introduced:
                  Stage 1 (same as Series C): features[6,7,8] + head; AdamW lr=3e-4,
                    CosineAnnealingLR(T_max=30), 30 epochs, patience=7.
                  Stage 2: additionally unfreezes features[5]; differential lr=3e-5;
                    CosineAnnealingLR(T_max=15), 15 epochs, patience=5.
                    Gate: only runs if Stage 1+TTA val AUC ≥ 0.752.
                    Reverts to Stage 1 weights if Stage 2 AUC regresses.
                TTA: 2-pass horizontal flip (score = mean sigmoid of orig + hflip).
                Ensemble: val-set grid search over w ∈ {0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.25, 0.30},
                    blending EfficientNet and CustomNetV2 scores.
   Result:      0.742 pub / 0.727 priv
   Notes:       Regression vs Series C single-stage (0.751). The Stage 2 gate (≥ 0.752)
                was likely not met in this run, so training stopped at Stage 1 with the
                same features[6-8] but now competing against a stronger optimizer baseline.
                The two-stage + TTA + ensemble infrastructure is foundational for all
                subsequent experiments; later attempts recover the AUC.

--

6. second_attempt_tuned.ipynb
   Changes:     WeightedRandomSampler added to the training DataLoader.
                Per-sample weights = inverse class frequency (1/class_count).
                DataLoader: shuffle=False (incompatible with sampler in PyTorch).
                All other FT components identical to second_attempt.
   Result:      0.770 pub / 0.744 priv  (+0.028 pub vs second_attempt)
   Notes:       The primary benefit is for CustomNetV2 training from scratch, but FT also
                improves substantially. More balanced mini-batches stabilise Stage 1
                convergence, allowing the Stage 2 gate (val AUC ≥ 0.752) to be met.
                WeightedRandomSampler remains in all subsequent attempts.

--

7. second_attempt_tuned_cache.ipynb
   Changes:     Pre-cached 224px images (CropByEye → Rescale(256) → CenterCrop(224),
                saved as .npy). DataLoader reads cached tensors directly at training time.
   Result:      0.742 pub / 0.728 priv  (−0.028 pub regression vs tuned)
   Notes:       EfficientNet Stage 1 best val AUC dropped from 0.779 (epoch 14 in tuned)
                to 0.748 (epoch 3). The cache applies CenterCrop at creation time, so
                RandomCrop(224) is never called during training. On a 2000-image dataset,
                per-epoch positional crop jitter (32px in both axes) is a primary source
                of augmentation regularisation. Without it the model memorises faster and
                peaks lower. Cache pipeline permanently abandoned.

8. third_attempt.ipynb  (Series G)
   Changes:     G-1: Rescale(256)+RandomCrop(224) → TVRandomResizedCrop(224,
                     scale=(0.65,1.0), ratio=(0.9,1.1)) in training pipeline.
                G-2: Label smoothing ε=0.05 for EfficientNet training only.
                     Hard {0,1} targets softened to {0.025, 0.975}.
                G-3: CustomNetV2 T_max=50 → T_max=25 (custom-only change).
   Result:      0.766 pub / 0.743 priv
   Notes:       Marginal improvement over tuned baseline (0.770 pub → 0.766). ResizedCrop
                combines scale jitter (65–100% of image area) with spatial jitter in one
                step, increasing per-epoch diversity. However it is later reverted in
                sixth_attempt because it creates a train/val crop mismatch that is harder
                to reason about than a fixed Rescale+CenterCrop eval pipeline. Label
                smoothing (G-2) is retained through sixth_attempt.

9. fourth_attempt.ipynb  (Series H) — CATASTROPHIC FAILURE
   Changes:     H-1: BenGraham local contrast normalisation added to both pipelines.
                H-2: 4-pass TTA: original + hflip + vflip + rot180.
                H-3: Label smoothing ε=0.05 for EfficientNet (unchanged from G-2).
   Result:      0.558 pub / 0.533 priv  (−0.193 pub from baseline; near-random)
   Root cause:  BenGraham formula applied to uint8 images without a dtype guard.
                np.clip(uint8_array, 0, 1) clamps every non-zero pixel to 1. Then:
                  4*1 − 4*GaussianBlur(1) + 128 ≈ 128 everywhere.
                Every training image was reduced to a uniform neutral-gray rectangle.
                The model trained on 2000 identical gray patches and converged to a
                near-constant output function.
   Notes:       H-2 (4-pass TTA) and H-3 (label smoothing) cannot be evaluated; their
                effects are entirely masked. All three changes are re-tested in
                fifth_attempt with the dtype bug fixed.

--

10. fifth_attempt.ipynb  (H-series corrected)
    Changes:    H-1 (fixed): dtype guard + circular background mask.
                  Guard:  if image.dtype == np.uint8: img_u8 = image
                          else: img_u8 = (np.clip(image, 0, 1) * 255).astype(np.uint8)
                  Mask:   pixels outside circle of radius 0.9*min(h,w)/2 set to gray 128.
                H-2: 4-pass TTA retained (orig + hflip + vflip + rot180).
                H-3: Label smoothing ε=0.05 for EfficientNet Stage 1 and Stage 2.
                Pipeline sanity-check cell added (asserts normalised mean ≠ 0.5).
                All other FT components identical to second_attempt_tuned.
    Result:     0.757 pub / 0.755 priv
    Notes:      Public slightly below second_attempt_tuned EfficientNet (−0.013), but
                private +0.011 (0.755 vs 0.744). The private uplift is consistent with
                BenGraham improving generalisation to the held-out test distribution:
                local contrast normalisation reduces reliance on global illumination cues
                that vary across imaging devices. 4-pass TTA is adopted permanently.

11. sixth_attempt.ipynb  (Series I)  *** NOT VALID — excluded by competition rules ***
    Changes:    I-1: DenseNet121 replaces EfficientNet-B0.
                     Head: Dropout(0.5) → Linear(1024, 1).
                     Stage 1 unfreezes features.denseblock4 + features.norm5 (~2.16M params,
                     vs 3.15M for EfficientNet features[6-8]).
                     AdamW lr=3e-4, wd=1e-2, CosineAnnealingLR(T_max=30), 30 epochs,
                     patience=7, label_smoothing=0.05.
                I-2: Stage 2 unfreezes features.denseblock3 + features.transition3.
                     Per-group LRs: denseblock3/transition3/denseblock4/norm5 at 3e-5;
                     classifier at 1e-4.
                     Scheduler: 3-epoch linear warmup (start_factor=0.1, end_factor=1.0)
                     followed by CosineAnnealingLR(T_max=12), composed via SequentialLR.
                     15 epochs, patience=5, label_smoothing=0.05.
                     Gate (≥ 0.752) and revert logic unchanged.
    Result:     0.781 pub / 0.781 priv  (+0.024 pub vs fifth_attempt EfficientNet)
    Notes:      DenseNet121 provides implicit regularisation via dense feature reuse:
                each layer receives feature maps from all preceding layers, so gradients
                flow to all layers simultaneously and no single layer can dominate.
                Fewer Stage 1 trainable params (2.16M) reduce overfitting on 2000 images.
                DenseNet121 (CheXNet) is the established backbone for medical imaging
                benchmarks and transfers reliably from ImageNet to fundus images.
                The 3-epoch linear warmup in Stage 2 prevents gradient spikes when newly
                unfrozen layers (pretrained but unadapted) first receive the fine-tuning LR.
                Both public and private AUC improved equally (+0.024/+0.026), confirming
                reduced overfitting rather than test-set memorisation.
                Sixth_attempt serves as the DenseNet121 reference for all Cris experiments.

--- Progressive Unfreezing Strategy (same for all backbones) ---

Stage 1:
  Unfreeze top layer(s) + head. Freeze entire backbone first.
    DenseNet121/169: features.denseblock4 + features.norm5
    ResNeXt50 / SE-ResNeXt50: layer4
  Optimizer:   AdamW, lr=3e-4, weight_decay=1e-2
  Scheduler:   CosineAnnealingLR(T_max=30)
  Epochs:      30 max, patience=7 (early stopping on val AUC)
  label_smoothing: 0.05

Stage 2:
  Load Stage 1 best weights. Additionally unfreeze one block deeper.
    DenseNet121/169: features.denseblock3 + features.transition3
    ResNeXt50 / SE-ResNeXt50: layer3
  Gate:        Skip Stage 2 if Stage 1+TTA val AUC < 0.752.
               Revert to Stage 1 weights if Stage 2 AUC is lower.
  Per-group LRs (DenseNet example):
    denseblock3 / transition3  (newly unfrozen): lr = 3e-5
    denseblock4 / norm5        (Stage 1 warm):   lr = 3e-5
    classifier head:                             lr = 1e-4
  Per-group LRs (ResNeXt example):
    layer3     (newly unfrozen): lr = 3e-5
    layer4     (Stage 1 warm):   lr = 3e-5
    fc head:                     lr = 1e-4
  Scheduler:   LinearLR(start_factor=0.1, end_factor=1.0, total_iters=3)
               then CosineAnnealingLR(T_max=12), composed via SequentialLR.
  Epochs:      15 max, patience=5, label_smoothing=0.05

--- Data Pipeline (380px) ---

Training:
  CropByEye(threshold=0.10, border=1)
  → BenGraham(sigmaX=10)          [4*img − 4*GaussianBlur(img,10) + 128; uint8 guard + circular mask]
  → Rescale(416)
  → TVRandomHorizontalFlip(p=0.5)
  → TVRandomRotation(180°)         [expanded from ±15° in sixth_attempt]
  → TVColorJitter(brightness=0.3, contrast=0.3, saturation=0.2, hue=0.05)
  → RandomCrop(380)                [up from 224 in sixth_attempt]
  → ToTensor → Normalize(mean=[0.485,0.456,0.406], std=[0.229,0.224,0.225])

Validation / Test:
  CropByEye → BenGraham → Rescale(416) → CenterCrop(380) → ToTensor → Normalize

--- Training Configuration ---

Loss:               BCEWithLogitsLoss(pos_weight = n_neg/n_pos ≈ 2.759)
                    (1468 No-DR / 532 DR in training split)
Sampler:            WeightedRandomSampler (inverse class frequency), shuffle=False
Batch sizes:        32 train / 128 val  (num_workers=0, WSL)
Label smoothing:    0.05 for all FT backbones
cudnn:              deterministic=True, benchmark=False
Memory:             FT models kept on CPU; moved to GPU one at a time during scoring
                    + torch.cuda.empty_cache() between backbones

--- TTA (Test-Time Augmentation) ---

4-pass: original + horizontal flip + vertical flip + rot180
Applied at both val (for Stage 2 gate / ensemble grid search) and test inference.
  s1 = sigmoid(model(inputs))
  s2 = sigmoid(model(torch.flip(inputs, dims=[3])))    # hflip
  s3 = sigmoid(model(torch.flip(inputs, dims=[2])))    # vflip
  s4 = sigmoid(model(torch.flip(inputs, dims=[2,3])))  # rot180
  out = (s1 + s2 + s3 + s4) / 4.0

--- Key Design Decisions (C-series changes in attempt_23/24) ---

(C-5)  Input resolution 224px → 380px.
       Microaneurysms are the earliest DR lesion (10–125 μm diameter). At 224px they
       are ~2px wide — below the reliable detection threshold of 3×3 conv filters.
       At 380px they are ~3–4px. Backbones are fully convolutional, so resolution
       change requires no architectural modification. Batch sizes reduced to 32/128
       to stay within GPU memory at larger feature maps.
       Scientific backing: Abramoff et al. (npj Digital Medicine, 2018) show that
       resolution is the primary driver of sensitivity for early DR lesion detection.
       Kaggle APTOS 2019 winning solutions uniformly used 380–456px inputs.

(C-6)  Rotation augmentation ±15° → ±180°.
       Fundus cameras can be held at any orientation; there is no canonical up/down.
       The previous ±15° window kept only 8% of the rotation space, artificially
       constraining training diversity. Full 360° rotation is a valid augmentation
       for every retinal image in the dataset.
       Scientific backing: Graham (Kaggle DR 2015 winner, 35k images) identified
       full 360° rotation as the single most important augmentation for fundus DR.
       Gargeya & Leng (Ophthalmology, 2017; ~1.7k training images, similar scale)
       also found random 360° rotation consistently improved AUC.

(C-7)  Load pre-trained backbone weights from attempt_23 (no retraining in attempt_24).
       All four backbones trained once at 380px / ±180°; attempt_24 performs only
       inference, ensemble grid search, and test score generation.


===========================================================================
FAILED FT APPROACHES
===========================================================================

The following directions were explored and abandoned. Each entry explains
what was tried, what happened, and the root cause where known.

--- Optimiser and Scheduler ---

1. SGD + StepLR (first_attempt through third_attempt EfficientNet-B0)
   What:     SGD with momentum=0.9, StepLR(step_size=7, gamma=0.1).
   Outcome:  Ceiling at 0.751 pub (attempt_with_c) after switching to AdamW+Cosine.
             Baseline SGD stalled at 0.698 pub.
   Why it failed: SGD requires careful momentum / weight decay tuning that is
             backbone-specific. AdamW's adaptive learning rates and decoupled
             weight decay are more robust for transfer learning on small datasets
             where different parameter groups have very different gradient scales.

--- Preprocessing Mistakes ---

2. CLAHE preprocessing — attempt_with_a_b
   What:     Contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalisation (LAB L-channel,
             clipLimit=2.0) inserted before CropByEye.
   Outcome:  0.711 pub public gain but 0.691 private — lower private than baseline.
   Why it failed: CLAHE alters the global intensity distribution. CropByEye uses a
             fixed threshold (0.10) on raw pixel intensity to detect the eye boundary.
             After CLAHE the background may exceed the threshold, producing incorrect
             crops and a noisier training set. Permanently banned.

3. BenGraham without uint8 guard — fourth_attempt (catastrophic)
   What:     BenGraham formula applied to uint8 images without checking dtype.
   Outcome:  0.558 pub / 0.533 priv — near-random performance.
   Why it failed: np.clip(uint8_array, 0, 1) clamps every non-zero pixel to 1.
             The weighted subtraction then maps every pixel to neutral gray 128.
             Training proceeds on a dataset of 2000 identical gray images.

--- Data Pipeline ---

4. Pre-cached image pipeline — second_attempt_tuned_cache
   What:     Images preprocessed and saved to disk at 224px. DataLoader reads
             .npy arrays directly, skipping CropByEye, BenGraham, and Rescale at
             train time. CenterCrop(224) used at cache creation → RandomCrop never called.
   Outcome:  0.742 pub (−0.028 regression vs tuned baseline).
   Why it failed: Per-epoch RandomCrop provides 32px of positional jitter in both
             axes. On a 2000-image training set this is the primary spatial regulariser.
             Removing it causes the model to converge faster to a lower peak.
             EfficientNet Stage 1 best val AUC: 0.748 (epoch 3) vs 0.779 (epoch 14).

5. 2-pass TTA only (second_attempt through fourth_attempt)
   What:     TTA limited to horizontal flip (score = mean of orig + hflip).
   Outcome:  AUC consistently lower than 4-pass TTA introduced in fifth_attempt.
   Why it failed: vflip and rot180 add meaningful inference diversity at no training
             cost. Fundus images have no strong vertical orientation prior, so vflip
             at inference is valid. 4-pass TTA is strictly better on this dataset.

--- Architecture Decisions ---

6. EfficientNet-B0 with three unfrozen blocks (features[6,7,8]) — attempt_with_c through fifth_attempt
   What:     EfficientNet-B0's compound-scaled architecture, unfreezing the top three
             feature blocks. Single-stage and two-stage variants both tested.
   Outcome:  Best EfficientNet result: 0.770 pub / 0.744 priv (second_attempt_tuned).
             DenseNet121 exceeded this by +0.011 pub / +0.037 priv in sixth_attempt.
   Why it failed: EfficientNet-B0 Stage 1 reached train AUC 0.919 at its best val
             checkpoint in fifth_attempt. Stage 2 memorised further (train AUC → 1.000)
             while val stagnated. 2000 training images are insufficient to regularise
             EfficientNet's compound-scaled architecture. DenseNet's dense connections
             provide stronger implicit regularisation through feature reuse.

7. DenseNet Stage 3 unfreeze — seventh_attempt
   What:     Extended progressive unfreezing to denseblock2 + transition2 (third stage).
   Outcome:  0.768 pub / 0.754 priv (−0.013 pub vs sixth_attempt two-stage).
   Why it failed: Two stages already unfreeze ~5.5M of DenseNet121's 6.95M parameters.
             Stage 3 adds further params that overfit on 2000 training images without
             introducing new representational capacity. Stages 1+2 are the optimal
             stopping point.

8. 8-pass TTA with rot90/rot270 — seventh_attempt
   What:     Extended 4-pass TTA with two additional 90°-rotation passes.
   Outcome:  AUC decreased for both FT and custom models vs 4-pass TTA.
   Why it failed: The BenGraham + rectangular crop pipeline is not invariant to 90°
             rotations. Images rotated 90° at inference fall outside the training
             distribution (all training images are upright within ±180°). The
             miscalibrated predictions from the rot90/rot270 passes degrade the mean.

9. EfficientNet-B4-NS as 5th backbone — attempt_23
   What:     tf_efficientnet_b4_ns (Noisy Student pretrained, via timm; feature dim = 1792)
             at 380px input. Stage 1 unfreezes blocks[6] + conv_head + bn2.
             Stage 2 additionally unfreezes blocks[5].
   Outcome:  CUDA OOM. 14.56 GiB GPU fully saturated at batch=16, 380px.
             Gradient checkpointing and batch size reductions did not resolve it.
   Why it failed: EfficientNet-B4 is substantially larger than B0. At 380px input the
             feature maps at each stage require too much GPU memory for a single-GPU
             setup. Scientific motivation was valid (APTOS 2019 top-10 used B3–B7),
             but hardware constraints make it incompatible with this setup.

10. SE-ResNeXt50 in final ensemble — attempt_24 grid search
    What:     All 4-backbone combinations including SE-ResNeXt50 were tested.
    Outcome:  Every 3-backbone blend containing SE-ResNeXt50 scored below the
              DenseNet121 + DenseNet169 + ResNeXt50 combination (val 0.8671).
    Why it failed: SE-ResNeXt50 val AUC (0.8341) is lower than ResNeXt50 (0.8549),
              and its predictions are positively correlated with ResNeXt50 (both are
              resnext family). Adding a weaker, correlated model dilutes the ensemble
              without adding complementary diversity.

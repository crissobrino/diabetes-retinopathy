Changes vs attempt_21.ipynb:

- (C-5) Input resolution increased to 380 px for all backbones.
  Rescale(256)->RandomCrop(224) replaced with Rescale(416)->
  RandomCrop/CenterCrop(380). DenseNet and ResNeXt backbones are
  fully convolutional and handle any input size via Global Average
  Pooling. Higher resolution preserves fine vessel detail:
  microaneurysms (the earliest DR lesion, 10-125 um diameter) are
  ~2 px wide at 224 px and ~3-4 px at 380 px — enough for conv
  filters to reliably detect them.

  Scientific backing: Abramoff et al. (npj Digital Medicine, 2018)
  show that resolution is a primary driver of sensitivity for early
  DR lesion detection. The Kaggle APTOS 2019 winning solutions
  uniformly used 380-456 px input on similarly-sized datasets.

- (C-6) Rotation augmentation expanded from ±15° to ±180°.
  Fundus images are rotationally symmetric: the relative orientation
  of the optic disc and macula depends on the camera geometry and
  patient head tilt, not anatomy. Any rotation is a valid fundus
  image. The previous ±15° window discarded 92% of valid orientations,
  artificially limiting the diversity seen during training.

  Scientific backing: Graham (Kaggle DR 2015 winner, dataset ~35k
  images) identified full 360° rotation as the single most important
  augmentation for fundus DR detection. Gargeya & Leng (Ophthalmology
  2017, ~1.7k training images — similar scale to this dataset) also
  found random rotation up to 360° consistently improved AUC.

- (C-7) Load pre-trained backbones from attempt_23 .pth files.
  The four backbones (DenseNet121, DenseNet169, ResNeXt50,
  SE-ResNeXt50) were trained in attempt_23 with C-5 and C-6 active.
  No retraining — weights loaded via MODELS_PATH constant.
  Only CustomNetV2 is trained from scratch in this notebook.

- MODELS_PATH / SAVE_PATH constants added for clean file I/O.
- TRAIN_BATCH=32, VAL_BATCH=128 (from 64/256) for safe 380px operation.
- FT models kept on CPU; moved to GPU one at a time during scoring.

Motivation for I-1 — replacing EfficientNet-B0 with DenseNet121:
  In fifth_attempt, EfficientNet-B0 Stage 1 reached train AUC 0.919 at its best
  validation checkpoint (epoch 7, val AUC 0.785), a train/val gap of 0.134.
  Stage 2 memorised further, reaching train AUC 1.000 while val stagnated.
  The 2000-image training set is too small for EfficientNet-B0's compound-scaled
  architecture to regularise naturally.

  DenseNet121 is a natural alternative for this regime:
  - Dense connections: each layer receives feature maps from all preceding layers
    within the block, creating implicit regularisation through feature reuse.
    The model is less likely to memorise because gradients flow to all layers
    simultaneously, preventing any single layer from dominating.
  - Established baseline for medical imaging: DenseNet121 (CheXNet) and its
    variants are the standard backbone for chest X-ray and DR detection benchmarks.
    The ImageNet weights transfer more reliably to fundus images than EfficientNet's
    compound-scaled features, which were optimised for a broader accuracy/efficiency
    trade-off.
  - Fewer trainable params in Stage 1: ~2.16M vs 3.15M for EfficientNet, reducing
    the number of parameters that can overfit the 2000 training images.

Motivation for I-2 — warmup scheduler:
  When Stage 2 unfreezes denseblock3 + transition3, the newly unfrozen layers
  start with ImageNet-pretrained weights that have not been adapted to the DR task.
  A sudden lr=3e-5 step at epoch 0 of Stage 2 can create a gradient spike that
  destabilises the Stage 1 features already adapted in denseblock4. The 3-epoch
  linear warmup (start_factor=0.1: effective lr=3e-6 at epoch 0, ramping to 3e-5
  at epoch 3) allows the new layers to align with the adapted features before the
  full learning rate is applied. After the warmup, CosineAnnealingLR(T_max=12)
  decays over the remaining 12 epochs.

  This replaces the flat CosineAnnealingLR(T_max=15) used in EfficientNet Stage 2
  (no warmup), which was sufficient for EfficientNet because features[5] was already
  partially adapted in that architecture's continuous feature pyramid.
